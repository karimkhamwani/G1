What the fills look like

The activity API caps history at 5,000 rows, which covered about 6.5 hours of September 3: 4,926 fills and 74 redemptions across 83 windows.

  • Buy only. Not a single sell in 4,926 fills, nor in a 490-fill sample from July 17. Exits happen by redemption after the window resolves.
  • 90% BTC, 10% ETH. Roughly every window: 503 of 575 BTC windows in the 48-hour scan below.
  • Both sides in 88% of windows. Median 29 fills per window, median fill $6, mean $15, bursts of up to 21 fills in one second. The average Up price plus average Down price comes to about 0.975 to 0.99, so paired shares are bought at a small discount to the $1 payout.
  • Maker and taker mixed. 60% of fills carry no fee (resting limit orders), 40% pay the taker fee of about 1.55 cents per share. Fees in the sample: $1,283 on $74,804 spent.
  • Spread across the window. Fill timing runs from 4 to 288 seconds after the open, median 111 seconds. Minute 0 is the busiest and loses money; minutes 1 to 3 make it.

Where the edge sits

Matching each completed window's redemption to its cost gives the true per-window result: +$1,132 on $73,506 deployed across 72 windows, a 1.5% margin after fees. 39 of 72 windows were profitable, the median window made $0.60, and the tails ran from −$477 to +$641. Scaled across a full day that reproduces the leaderboard figure of about $10K on $919K of volume.

2. Kelly sizing for a binary contract

Buying a share at price p that pays $1 with true probability q, the growth-optimal fraction of bankroll is

f* = (q − p) / (1 − p)

Payoff odds are b = (1 − p) / p and the general form f = (bq − (1 − q)) / b reduces to the line above. With a taker fee, use the effective price p + fee (about 0.015 here). A negative result means no bet.

The whale's actual behaviour reads as fractional Kelly on a hedged book. Gross deployment of about $3K per window is 15% to 20% of bankroll, which looks like full Kelly or more. But Up and Down share counts differ by only about 20% on median, so net directional exposure is closer to $600, or 3% to 4% of bankroll. That is quarter to half Kelly on the actual bet, with the remainder locked into near-riskless pairs. It explains the $48 drawdown.

Why to shade below the table

  • Effective sample size is windows, not fills. All 30 fills in a window share one outcome, so 4,800 fills are 72 independent trials. A 52% hit rate on 72 trials has a standard error near 6 points, which spans the entire edge. Half or quarter Kelly is the textbook response.
  • Correlated bets. BTC and ETH windows open together and resolve on the same macro move. Size them as one bet.
  • q must come from a live model of distance from the window open and time remaining. The bucket table summarises that model; it cannot replace it.
  • Fees flip marginal edges. A 0.25 contract with a 25% hit rate is break-even before fees and a loser after them.

Rule of thumb: size each side at a quarter of (q − p − fee) / (1 − p), cap total exposure so a full loss on the unhedged tilt costs under 2% of bankroll, and pause when the rolling 200-window hit rate falls inside the noise band of the implied price.

Findings

  • The payoff is asymmetric and the win rate does not cover it. Mean entry 0.80: a win pays about $1.00 on 5 shares, a full loss costs $4, a stop costs about $2.35. Break-even needs roughly 80% winners across all entries. The run delivered 65%.
  • The stop-loss does not protect capital. The trigger is a 0.45 ask, but after the 2-second modelled latency the fills average 0.33 (39 fills at 0.4, 22 at 0.3, 21 at 0.2). Holding the 89 stopped positions to resolution would have lost $187.60 instead of $184.40. The stop is neutral in aggregate: the problem is the 29% of entries that reverse, not the exit rule.
  • Model confidence carries no information. Stop-out rate by confidence bucket: 0.70–0.75: 33%; 0.75–0.80: 31%; 0.80–0.85: 27%; 0.85–0.90: 36%; 0.90–0.95: 26%; 0.95–1.00: 28%. Held positions win about 91% regardless. The fair-value gate is not separating good entries from bad ones; the book price alone is doing the work.
  • Regime is the strongest filter available. Trending: 85 markets, +$19.93, 15% stopped. Choppy: 224 markets, −$91.99, 34% stopped. 76 of 89 stops were in choppy regime.
  • Expensive entries have no edge. Held entries at 0.70–0.89 beat implied odds by 10 to 13 points. Entries at 0.90 and above won 95% to 96%, which is the implied rate, and netted about zero. That is 90 cents at risk to win 5 to 10.
  • Entry timing barely matters for held positions (89% to 100% win in every minute), which again points at the stops as the whole story.

Data quality problems in the run

  • 81 of 220 held markets settled by the spot-proxy fallback, not the official result. Those show an 83% win rate against 96% for officially settled ones, and 17 were decided by a move under 0.02%, where the Chainlink 60-second TWAP can disagree with the spot feed. Treat about $16 of P&L as unverified.
  • The $40 daily-loss halt tripped and generated 16,024 "halted" vetoes, yet trading resumed. Confirm whether the halt resets at UTC midnight by design.
  • The 4-market concurrency cap vetoed 24,583 signals. With five assets running, the bot mostly trades whichever windows opened first, not the strongest signals.
  • Fees are zero in paper. Live, the observed taker fee is about 1.5 cents per share and a stopped trade pays it on both legs. That is another $25 to $30 off this run.
  • Fill latency is a fixed 2,027 ms assumption in paper mode. Real fills at 0.80 with FOK orders crossing two ticks will sometimes miss entirely and sometimes fill worse.

The contrast. The profitable wallets buy at 0.49 to 0.58, hedge both sides, and never sell; their own fills show that buys above 0.50 lose slightly and cheap buys carry the edge. This bot buys one-sided at 0.80 and sells into weakness. It is systematically taking the side of the trade the whales are content to give away.

5. What to change, in order

  1. Restrict entries to 0.70–0.85 and skip 0.90 and above. The high bucket contributed nothing while carrying most of the downside per share.
  2. Trade only the trending regime, or cut choppy size to the exchange minimum. This alone would have turned −$72 into roughly +$20 on the same signals.
  3. Drop SOL, XRP and BNB until BTC alone is reliably profitable. Each alt lost between $14 and $28 over the run.
  4. Remove the stop or set it near 0.60 so the fill lands above 0.45. At 5 shares the stop is not saving capital, and at current parameters it realises the worst of both worlds.
  5. Fix the official-result fetch so the fallback rarely fires. Right now 37% of held P&L rests on a spot proxy that disagrees with the oracle near the strike.
  6. Then, and only then, consider the whale's structure: resting bids on both sides below fair value, taker fills on the cheap side after an overreaction, no exits before resolution, and Kelly-fraction sizing of the net tilt.
